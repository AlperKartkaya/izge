# DEMO_SCRIPT.md
## Izge Demo Script

## 1. Demo goal
The goal of the demo is to make the audience understand that Izge is:
- local-first,
- memory-native,
- evidence-backed,
- capable of both reactive reasoning and proactive detection,
- more than search,
- more than chat.

The audience should leave with one impression:

> “This system reconstructs organizational memory and turns it into grounded decision intelligence.”

---

## 2. Demo strategy
The demo should emphasize:
1. real artifacts,
2. local/company-controlled processing,
3. structured memory reconstruction,
4. contradiction and drift visibility,
5. next-action guidance,
6. inspectability and evidence.

Do not spend too much time on:
- raw architecture detail,
- generic AI capabilities,
- broad feature lists.

---

## 3. Canonical demo flow

### Step 1 — Show the workspace is real
Very briefly show:
- artifacts exist,
- ingestion happened,
- memory objects and signals exist,
- the workspace is not empty or fake.

### Step 2 — Query-driven scenario
Ask a real product/roadmap question such as:
- “Why was the self-serve launch delayed?”
- “Does the current launch plan conflict with prior history?”
- “Which assumptions behind this plan are now stale?”
- “What should the team do next?”

Show:
- direct answer,
- why this answer,
- evidence,
- contradiction/drift if relevant,
- recommended next action.

### Step 3 — Drill down once
Open:
- a supporting artifact,
- a related memory object,
- or a contradiction pair.

Show that the system is not bluffing.

### Step 4 — Proactive ingestion scenario
Submit a new artifact such as:
- a conflicting weekly update,
- a revised launch assumption,
- a reopening decision note.

Then show that Izge:
- detects contradiction and/or assumption drift,
- links it to prior history,
- produces grounded next-action guidance.

### Step 5 — Close with continuity / trust
Optionally mention continuity use cases such as preventing repeated work across intern cycles by surfacing prior relevant reports and outcomes.

Close with:
> Izge does not just search company knowledge. It reconstructs decision history, reveals where it breaks, and helps teams decide what comes next.

---

## 4. What to emphasize
- hidden structure inside organizational history,
- evidence-backed reasoning,
- contradiction and drift as product-native capabilities,
- local-first trust,
- inspectability,
- coherence between admin preparation and member reasoning.

## 5. What not to emphasize
- generic chat behavior,
- speculative future simulation,
- every admin feature,
- low-level infra unless asked.

## 6. Demo rule
If the audience leaves thinking “nice AI interface,” the demo failed.
If they leave thinking “this is a new product category for institutional memory,” the demo succeeded.
